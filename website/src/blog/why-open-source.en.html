Think about what passes through your mail app in a year. Contracts, invoices, medical results, password resets for every account you own, and the things you only say to people you trust. No other program on your computer sees that much of your life.

Most mail apps ask you to take their word for what they do with it. Regards does not. Every line of it is public, and this is why.

Trust you can check

A privacy policy is a promise. Source code is a fact. When we say that your mail travels between your computer and your mail provider and nowhere else, you do not have to believe us: the code that opens every connection is there to read. So is the absence of anything else. There is no telemetry in Regards and no server of ours for it to call.

You may never read a line of it yourself. It still protects you, because anyone can: researchers, journalists, competitors, the curious. An app that can be inspected at any moment has to behave at every moment.

It matters twice when an AI is involved

Regards lets your AI agent work in your mailbox: read, file, draft, and send once you approve. That is a lot of power to hand over, and “trust us” is not a good enough answer.

The agent interface is open like the rest. You can see exactly what an agent is allowed to do at each level, that sending always waits for you, and that every action is recorded and can be undone. The limits are in the code, where they can be verified, not in a brochure.

Your mail app cannot be taken away

Apps get bought, repriced, filled with ads, or shut down. When that happens to a closed app, its users have no say.

Regards is published under the Apache-2.0 and MIT licences, which give everyone the right to use, copy, change and share it, for good. There is no account with us to close and no server of ours that your copy depends on. If we stopped tomorrow, the Regards you have would keep working, and anyone could carry it on.

Free, and honest about why

Regards costs nothing: every feature, all your addresses, no trial, no ads. It can be free because it has no servers to pay for. Your computer does the work, and for AI you connect the agent you already have.

One optional paid service is planned for version 1.1: sending while your computer is off, which does need a server. The app itself stays free, and stays open.

Built in the open, not only published

Open source can mean a pile of code thrown over a wall. We mean more than that.

  • The decisions are public. Why Regards is built the way it is, is written down in the repository, one decision at a time.
  • The measurements are public. When we say Regards is fast, the test and its raw results are there, including the line we lost on the first run.
  • The mistakes are public. Bugs are reported, discussed and fixed where everyone can see.
  • The door is open. Report a problem, ask for a feature, or send a change. Here is how to contribute.

Read it, build it, change it

The whole of Regards is at github.com/lucienPochon/regards: the app, the mail engine and the agent interface. Download it and use it, or clone it and build your own. Either way, it is yours.
